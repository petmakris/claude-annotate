The browser first asks the DNS resolver where the site lives. [[point: step s2]] The resolver answers with an address, and I made this example up.
[[point: step s3]] Next the browser opens a connection to that address, on port 443. That costs one round trip.
[[point: step s4]] Then it says hello and sends its half of a secret key. [[point: step s5]] The server replies with its certificate and its own half.
[[point: step s6]] The browser checks that the certificate names this site, is in date, and comes from a trusted issuer. [[key: the certificate proves who the server is]]
Both sides now mix the two halves into the same key. [[point: step s7]] The browser sends its first encrypted byte, which is the web request itself. [[key: the key is built, never sent]] [[point: outcome]] So the whole setup costs two round trips.
